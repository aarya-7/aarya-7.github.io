It's Never Really About The Data

What actually gets me isn't the data itself, it's human behavior. I've always liked watching why people do what they do, the cues that flip someone from a yes to a no, patterns behind a decision they probably didn't even notice they were making. Psychology's part of that too, it always felt like the same question, just asked from a different perspective. Data's just where I get to chase that properly, with real evidence instead of a guess.

I do this outside of work too. I'll catch myself noticing how someone lives, the habits they've built without thinking about it, the small choices that add up into something bigger, and I don't really turn it off. Why does someone stop using something they used to like, why do they come back after leaving, why does one nudge work when an almost identical one doesn't, I think every one of those questions has a person behind it, doing something for a reason, and figuring out that reason is the part I enjoy the most. A few places this has shown up. A funnel of 1.76 million sessions where the real drop happened earlier than anyone expected, not at checkout. A churn model across 500 SaaS accounts where the customers we lost turned out to be worth more than the ones we kept. A revenue anomaly system built on a 409-day baseline that had to explain itself, not just raise a flag.

Outside Of Work

Outside of data, cooking takes up a good chunk of my time, especially Asian cuisines. Travel's the other constant, I'll go wherever's next on the list before I've fully unpacked from the last one. I read constantly, I'm always up for a chess game, and football or cricket will pull my attention just as fast. Throw in a conversation about why people behave the way they do, and that's four completely unrelated rabbit holes I'm happy to fall into. I also dance, sing, and occasionally end up in theatre, which turns out to be a decent way to study people too, just with worse lighting and higher stakes. And when I actually want my brain off instead of on, it's food or FIFA on PS5, no analysis, no pattern-hunting, just trying not to concede in the 90th minute again.
