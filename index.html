ABOUT ME

I work in data analytics, and the thread running through everything I do is customer behavior, specifically why people stop doing the thing they were doing. Churn, cart abandonment, a sudden revenue drop, it's all the same instinct to me, finding the exact moment things broke, and why.

Sometimes that moment is earlier than expected. On an e-commerce funnel project, everyone assumed the biggest drop-off was at checkout, it wasn't, the real collapse happened much earlier, at the point where browsing was supposed to turn into intent. On a SaaS churn project, the pattern that stuck with me wasn't the churn rate itself, it was that the customers we lost were worth more on average than the ones we kept, concentrated in mid-sized accounts that were big enough to matter but small enough that nobody was personally watching them. That reframed churn for me as an attention problem, not just a product problem. And on a revenue anomaly system I built, flagging the weird days was never the hard part, explaining them was, so the system didn't just say revenue dropped, it said which category caused it.

Currently exploring Data Analyst, Business Analyst and Product Analyst roles.

RECENT PROJECTS

E-commerce Funnel Analysis

Analyzed 1.76M sessions in PostgreSQL to locate the primary revenue leak at View to Cart (2.21% conversion), not checkout. Returning visitors converted at 3x the rate of new users, informing 3 A/B tests delivered across 4 Tableau dashboards.

GMV Anomaly Detection & Attribution

Built a Python and PostgreSQL pipeline that flags, decomposes, and narrates GMV anomalies automatically, surfacing 12 revenue events across 18 months. Used MAD-based z-scores for robust detection and constrained an LLM to generate auditable What/Why/Action briefs with no manual query.

SaaS Retention & Churn Analytics

Quantified $9.9M paid MRR against an 8.02% churn rate across 500 accounts, isolating the 21 to 50 seat segment ($165K churned MRR) as the concentration point. Risk-band scoring validated against actual outcomes: HIGH-risk churned at 50% vs. 15% for MEDIUM accounts.

Controlled Experiments Are an Operating System, Not a Feature

Kohavi et al.'s paper on controlled experiments is a case for why you trust the experiment over the expert, every time. A coupon code field wiped out roughly 90% of checkout revenue, a more usable 5-star widget cut feedback rates by 90%, and Amazon's cart recommendations nearly never shipped because a marketing leader blocked them without data. Running experiments is not a feature you bolt onto product development, it is the operating system underneath every decision worth making.
